# Visual SEO (search, keywords, metadata)

This covers how Instagram's search and classification read a post, and where keywords must go. It's read by `caption-seo-writer`, `carousel-builder` and `profile-auditor`. (It replaces the former `skills/caption-seo-writer/references/seo-zones.md`.)

**Citations:** `[F<part>.<n>]` · `[U.n]` · `[U.chN]`. **Everything is a `book-claim`.** Hashtag ruling: C-2.

## 1. The system reads everything
- **Multimodal reading:** the book says the platform's model analyses frames, objects, on-screen text and speech to decide what a post is about and who it's for [U.ch1], [U.ch3], [U.117].
- **Words must match what's shown:** if they contradict the visuals, ranking drops [U.ch3].
- **Speech is transcribed:** keywords spoken early classify the video [U.40], [U.143], [F2.16], [F2.36].
- **On-screen text is read (OCR)** [F3.35], [U.ch3].
- **Background objects count:** props and setting signal niche and level [U.154], [U.conclusion.2].
- **Visible items make the platform's AI suggest better comments to viewers** ("What's this book called?") [U.30].
- **Image emotion:** photos are classified by the emotion they convey and shown to matching audiences. Positive emotion spreads further [F3.25].

## 2. Keyword golden zones [U.ch3]
| Zone | Tactic | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Name field (profile) | "Name \| niche keyword" | [U.6], [F4.2] |
| Caption, first 5 words | Front-load the primary keyword, naturally | [U.ch3], [U.6] |
| Alt text | ~100-character literal scene description with keywords | [U.ch3], [U.6] |
| On-screen text | Clear, readable font; include the keyword | [U.ch3], [F3.35] |
| Spoken audio | Say the keyword clearly in the first seconds | [U.40], [U.143] |

**Consistency check** (run by `caption-seo-writer`): the keyword appears in the voiceover (first ~5 s), the on-screen text and the caption's first 5 words.

## 3. Hashtags
Hashtags have been demoted, and keyword search now leads [U.6], [U.143], [F2.42]. **Ruling C-2:** 0–3 specific hashtags (niche + place). Local hashtags help local discovery [F3.22].

## 4. Caption content
- Match the caption to the video; this beats random hashtags [F2.42].
- A long, useful caption can hold people past the 10-second Feed "stop" [U.28].
- Phrase it so people save and revisit [U.105], and send it to a specific person [U.36].
- End with a question or open prompt for conversation depth [U.60], [U.123].
- Tie the content to a real buyer need, so it reaches shopping-minded audiences [F2.51]. Keep the CTA non-"buy now" (C-9, E-3).
- Professional framing ("share this with your team") signals business content, which the book links to desktop/business viewing [F2.1].

## 5. Location
- Precise real place tags (the venue, not the city) surface posts on the Friends Map and in local discovery [U.27], [F3.22].
- Location in the bio/profile feeds geographic circles [F4.11].

## 6. Language and reach beyond it
- The audience's dialect gives a local boost [F1.37], [F3.41].
- Auto-translation for Reels (clear audio needed) [U.9]; text translation in Stories [F3.18].

## 7. Tagging, collabs and cross-posting
- Tag partners, tools or brands that genuinely took part [U.66].
- Collab posts merge audiences into both Explore pages [U.145].
- Cross-post to Facebook and Threads via Accounts Center for a larger "signal cluster" [U.39].

## 8. Tools the book lists
Meta Edits (clean export), Mavic.ai (alt text / SEO captions), CreatorFlow (reply automation → conversation depth), and the Muse Spark visual check [U.ch8]. Treat these as book mentions, not endorsements. Check that each exists and what it costs before use.
