# Profile Architecture

This covers the profile as a conversion page: what a visitor sees in the first seconds, and how that becomes a follow, a profile browse, or a DM or link tap. It's read by `profile-auditor`.

## 1. The 3-second follow decision
A visitor decides to follow or leave within about 3 seconds, based on how the profile looks, not on the content [F4.1], [U.ch5]. The photo, the name field and the **first bio line** must together give a clear reason to follow [F4.1]. The book says a clear bio can raise visitor-to-follower conversion by about 40% [U.ch5] (book's figure). The profile is also a "digital ID card" the system reads to decide who to suggest you to [U.ch5].

## 2. Name field and handle
- The **name field** (display name, not @handle) is searchable. Put profession or niche keywords there: "Name | niche keyword" [F4.2], [U.6], [U.ch3 golden zones].
- Keep it readable; one keyword phrase beats stuffing.
- A leading symbol/emoji in the name for a "motion" ranking boost [F4.24] is a low-confidence claim. Optional at most; never at the cost of readability.
- Changing the name field every 30–45 days to be "re-tested" for discovery [F4.41] conflicts with a fixed identity [U.54]. See C-14. Default: **stable**.
- Clear real names and brands raise first-visit trust [F3.13].

## 3. Profile photo
- A face looking at the camera, bright and clear, on a solid bright background; no side angles or group shots [F4.3], [U.ch5].
- **Faceless brands:** use a high-contrast logo mark that's legible at 110 px. Choose a background colour that contrasts with competitors' profile photos [F4.36] (optional; the book suggests a monthly change, but keep it stable per C-14).

## 4. Bio
Instagram allows 150 characters. The formulas below can be combined; pick one structure.

- **Core formula** [F4.4]:
  1. What you offer (value)
  2. For whom
  3. How / proof
  4. A call to message or tap the link
- **First line = "I help you [goal] via [method]"**, using specialty keywords (a complete, clear bio ranks better in search) [U.ch5].
- **Speak to one visitor directly** ("Your content deserves…", not "I share…") [F4.21], [F4.28] (a line that names their pain).
- **Emotion over information** [F4.33]. **The 3-line story variant:** before → after → invitation [F4.19].
- **A touch of real-life imperfection** beats corporate polish [F4.27].
- **Last line = a tap trigger:** link, DM keyword or follow line [F4.13], [F4.30], [F4.31]. It must be honest (no invented surprises; see §11).
- **Location** in the bio/profile for local audiences [F4.11] (skip it for global digital products).
- **Unusual Unicode symbols as bullets** (⌬ ⟡ ➸) to stand out [F4.20]. Optional; check they render on both iOS and Android.
- **A/B test two bios** over two periods; compare profile visits and DMs in Insights [F4.23], [F4.35].

## 5. Link, buttons and DM intent
- **Link:** a trusted, clean destination. Link hubs (Linktree etc.) or your own domain are fine; never competitor apps or suspicious hosts, which the book says can reduce visibility [F3.2]. A readable link name helps [F4.9].
- The bio link is the **one place** a link belongs. Posts should use DM or comment keywords (E-3).
- **Taps are "profile intent" signals:** link, message and email/phone buttons [F4.13].
- **Contact buttons relabelled as offers** ("Free consultation") [F4.22], [F4.34] only if that offer really exists. Otherwise it's deceptive (E-4/E-5).
- **Pinned first comment** pointing to the link or gift for visitors who won't tap the bio [F4.42].
- **DM trigger lines** ("message me the word …") raise DM volume [F4.31], [F4.44]. Use a real keyword that sends a real thing.
- Encourage active followers to share or mention the page, which feeds "suggested accounts" [F4.43], [F3.4].

## 6. Highlights
- Highlights act as social proof and a "trial experience" [F4.5].
- **Recommended order** (merging [F4.5] and [F4.26]):
  1. Strongest proof / best content (a "start here" or intro)
  2. What you offer
  3. Results / testimonials (**only real ones**)
  4. Tips / education
  5. Contact / how to buy
- **The first story in the first highlight** is a mini ad of 15 seconds or less: who you are → the old problem → how you solved it → how you help → follow [F4.25], [U.ch5].
- **Unified cover design** strengthens the visual identity [F4.5]. Refresh covers and add new stories so highlights re-surface [U.38].
- A "wait for it…" beat inside a highlight adds 2–3 s of dwell [F4.37].
- A curiosity highlight ("Coming soon", a teaser) [F4.14] only if something is genuinely coming.

## 7. Pinned posts
Pin 3 [F4.7], [U.ch5]:
1. Your top-performing or most representative video
2. Proof / a big achievement / social proof (**real**)
3. A free gift or lead magnet (e.g. a guide, a quiz)

The pins work as a shop window: instant credibility, and they invite browsing [U.ch5]. Before real proof exists, use the clearest "what this is" post in slot 2.

## 8. The grid
- The first 9 tiles are the instant impression [F4.6]. Balance three things: human presence or brand signature, valuable information, and posts with engagement [F4.6].
- Avoid three look-alike text tiles in a row, and avoid a fully uniform grid; nothing stands out [F4.6].
- Deliberate contrast tiles (a calm tile next to a loud one) create a pattern break [F4.15]. Colour-block the grid, then break it with one post [F4.38] (the source labels this "Notes" but describes grid colour; treated as a grid tactic).
- **Reel covers built for the grid:** face (or brand mark) + a prominent keyword + one dominant warm colour [F4.17].
- **Archive weak or off-brand old posts.** Archiving hides them from the public and, per the book, from the algorithm's view [F4.8].

## 9. Time on profile
Instagram tracks seconds spent on the profile after a Reel [F4.10], [F1.41]. Things that extend it:
- a bio line pointing into highlights ("Open the first highlight…") [F4.10]
- intro highlights [F4.25]
- pinned posts [F4.7]
- series content ("part 2 on my page") [U.120]

## 10. New accounts and maintenance
- **New account:** the book claims a short test boost. Post in a consistent pattern, and don't delete posts or edit covers or captions after publishing [F3.16]. Accounts under 10k supposedly get higher reach rates [U.31].
- **Set the profile up completely before the first Reel**, so the first visitors meet a finished page (§1).
- **Account type:** Creator is generally favoured for reach [F2.30], [F2.3] (Business if you rely on shop/ads tools).
- **Fixed identity:** one consistent visual style and tone [U.54]. Only small bio tweaks, and A/B tests run deliberately [F4.23].
- **Bio refresh cadence claims** (every 48–72 h [F4.18]; a full profile overhaul every 10 days [F4.32]) are low-confidence and conflict with U.54 (C-14). Not recommended by default.
- **Notes feature:** a short daily line in Notes (shown in DMs) keeps the profile "alive" [F4.40]. Content must be honest (see §11).

## 11. Excluded and low-confidence
| Tactic | Source | Ruling |
|---|---|---|
| Invisible white keyword text in the bio | [F4.16] | **Excluded** (E-7) |
| Fake scarcity lines ("only 3 spots", "link active 2 days") when untrue | [F4.29], [F4.12] | **Excluded** (E-4). Use only real limits |
| "Temporary confusion" bio ("Closed system — first 25 only") | [F4.12] | Excluded if untrue (E-4); a genuine teaser is fine |
| Relabelled phone button promising something that doesn't exist | [F4.22], [F4.34] | Excluded unless real (E-5) |
| Rare foreign script / "linguistic noise" to seem mysterious | [F4.39] | Not recommended: hurts clarity [U.41] and search |
| Coded message decoded only by DM ("SBRA — key for those interested") | [F4.44] | Allowed only if the DM delivers something real |
| Frequent renames and full refreshes | [F4.18], [F4.32], [F4.41] | C-14: stable by default |
